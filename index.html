03 Mid-Market operating model

A very challenging commitment. SOT kept changing. No execution owner.

Why I chose this example

It is the closest structural match to what Mid-Market needs now: no stable SOT, no execution owner, numerous asks without a stack rank, and leaders pulled into blockers weekly.

This was not a delivery problem. It was an operating-model problem—the kind of ambiguity a Principal TPM must walk into and structure before someone else defines the system.

The planning reality

  • 70–90+ IYPs with blank fields, no stack rank, and no frozen source of truth
  • A commitment requested before technical alignment
  • Feature-level ticket explosion with conditional accepts and weak follow-through
  • Leadership pulled into blockers at roughly one escalation every week

Why I was brought in

Lead SPOC across Business Platform and Mid-Market.

I owned intake, escalations, leadership communications, portfolio status, and TPM assignments—while driving cutline negotiation and IYP discipline with the core team.

184+ asks disciplinedLead SPOC3-person core model
03 Mid-Market operating model

The operating system I built

Prioritize. Shift left. Assign E2E ownership. Run the hotspots.

01

Stack-rank the portfolio

Created the first stack-ranked list and negotiated a realistic cutline.

02

Shift alignment left

Required technical deep dives and designs before acceptance: workshops → IYPs → cutline.

03

Organize by capability

Reframed ticket sprawl into 3–6 product tracks, one IYP per feature, and reuse over rebuild.

04

Name E2E owners

Assigned a TPM POC per feature group, with each TPM owning Business Platform execution end to end.

05

Run one leadership system

Connected the Evergreen Deck, hotspot reviews, program review, and Super Scrum into one escalation path.

03 Mid-Market operating model

Pushback stories

The system did not arrive unchallenged.

Three moments where I had to earn adoption—not simply design the mechanism.

SituationThe resistanceAction taken
Stack rank + cutline70–90+ IYPs, no shared priority, and Business Platform decisions happening in silos. Business wanted commitments faster; Dev Managers resisted losing local decision autonomy. Negotiated the timeline with Mid-Market BizOps, created one cross-BP accept/decline process, and used shared criteria plus technical input so tradeoffs were made together—not team by team.
Tech review before commitmentTeams were being asked to commit before technical validation. The review step was seen as slowing an already compressed timeline. Introduced technical discussions before commitment and created a middle ground: conditional acceptance tied to tech review and an explicit code-contribution model.
Hotspots before escalationIssues went directly to the main SoS and pulled leaders into blockers weekly. Teams and leaders were accustomed to escalating directly and questioned another meeting. Redirected the first escalation to the hotspot forum, secured leaders from both organizations, resolved issues before SoS, and demonstrated a ~6× escalation reduction.

The finished model was earned—not simply designed.
